# Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric when comparing mathematical modeling contest problems. Score every dimension from 1 to 5. Higher is always better for selecting the problem.

Default total score is the unweighted sum out of 40. If two problems differ by 2 points or less, treat them as effectively tied and decide by the strongest team-fit or time-risk argument.

| Dimension | 5 | 3 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 题意清晰度 | Tasks, variables, constraints, and deliverables are clear | Some ambiguity, but a workable interpretation is possible | Core task or deliverable is unclear |
| 数据可用性 | Data is complete, readable, and directly tied to the questions | Data needs cleaning or interpretation but is usable | Data is missing, inaccessible, or poorly tied to the task |
| 建模可解释性 | Natural models are easy to explain in a contest paper | Models are explainable with careful framing | Likely methods are opaque or hard to justify |
| 代码实现难度 | Can be implemented with common Python/Excel tools | Requires moderate algorithms, tuning, or file handling | Requires difficult engineering, special software, or fragile parsing |
| 结果可验证性 | Results can be checked against data, constraints, or templates | Some validation is possible but incomplete | Hard to know whether results are correct |
| 论文表达空间 | Clear story, figures, tables, sensitivity analysis, and conclusions | Paper can be written, but the story needs shaping | Hard to produce a convincing paper narrative |
| 时间风险 | Low risk for contest schedule; v1 can be finished quickly | Manageable with disciplined scope | High chance of getting stuck before usable results |
| 队伍匹配度 | Strong fit for current skills, tools, and background | Partial fit with learnable gaps | Major mismatch with team ability or available time |

## Scoring Notes

- Use the default team profile when the user provides no team information.
- For time risk, 5 means low risk and 1 means high risk.
- Give a short reason for every score of 1 or 5.
- Do not use decimal scores unless the user asks for finer ranking.
- Report both total score and the decisive risks; do not hide a critical blocker behind a good total score.
